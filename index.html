Objective

Tell the partner what to do, before telling him what we know

The CSP app is an execution surface. He acts as a sub-agent of Wiom, so the app's job is to produce predictable outcomes — and every decision he takes should come back to us as a signal we can either verify or learn from.

He can accept or decline a booking at the start, and he should have the information he needs to make that call properly. In the absence of it he may decline, and a decline for want of information is a booking lost for no reason. Once he has accepted, we want him to complete the job rather than leave it mid-way; and where he genuinely cannot, we want to know why, because a stated reason is worth far more to us than an abandonment.

Acceptance is the point that divides the journey. Up to it, saying no costs him nothing and the only thing that matters is that he is deciding with full information. From that point his install SLA is engaged, so the app's job changes: it should help him finish, and where he wants out it should offer him a way to save the job before it is given up.

The exceptions belong to him too — if the customer cancels, that is not his failure and it should not count against him.

He is worried about his install SLA because it is tied to his earnings, and that worry can work against us. When a customer does not pick up the phone he sometimes cancels the connection, which costs him the very SLA he is protecting — and he may not know that. Wherever his instinct and his interest diverge like this, the app should make the honest action the safe one, and say so plainly.

For this phase we have chosen to manage the customer's expectation on date adherence rather than enforce a particular time on the CSP. A date change that does not hurt his SLA should encourage him to tell us the real install date, which lets us set the customer's expectation in time — instead of a customer waiting and being told about the delay after the fact.

The journey is also a repeat task. He will do it many times a week, so the accepting path should stay fast and free of ceremony; whatever friction the design needs belongs on the exit, which is rare and is where jobs are actually lost. Where we do need to check something he has told us, we should do it without making him feel he is under suspicion.

From Wiom's side the goal is simple: as many installs completed as possible, and none lost to lack of clarity or coordination. The design principle throughout is predictability for the CSP — he is the key persona for every call we make.

Decided

Rules the design is built on

These came out of the objective-setting discussion and drove every screen.

Install SLA

  • Declining before accepting — no SLA impact.
  • Customer cancels — no SLA impact.
  • Accepts, then declines himself — SLA impact, depending on the reason.
  • Does not act and the lead is taken back — SLA impact.

Acceptance

  • Acceptance happens on the booking screen — the ✓. The SLA clock starts there.
  • After the ✓ he sees the date and the customer, then assigns to a team or to himself.
  • P41 starts the moment he receives a booking and stops the moment he accepts.

Date changes

  • Free up to n times, n = 2.
  • SLA is measured against the CSP's latest proposed date, not the customer's original one.
  • Once the grace period is over, the lead is taken back.
  • A date changed by the CSP is final — it is not confirmed with the customer. The customer is told the install day has moved for technical reasons.

Calls

  • The CSP should call the customer at least once himself, and escalate to Wiom only if the customer is unreachable.
  • When Wiom reaches the customer, the CSP also gets a call — the moment he picks up, the two are connected.

Verification

  • Verify customer claims, but never make the CSP feel untrusted — frame it as "we will try to revive this and call the customer, you can wait."
  • If it still dies after Wiom has tried, it does not count against him. No penalties.

Reasons

  • Keep only the most relevant four, plus an escape option.
  • Every reason opens a recovery before the booking is given up.

Decline and reassignment

  • A connection is never removed — it is taken back. The ticket stays in My Tasks with an updated status.
  • A system re-triggered booking can be reversible if no other CSP takes it. out of scope

Money

  • Wiom intends to pay more when the location is farther than typical, and this must be visible before he decides — that is what makes him put in the extra effort. out of scope

System rules

  • P41 — acceptance timer, ends at accept, not at technician assigned.
  • P74 — grace timer post slot, runs as it does today.
  • Quality — after acceptance, a decline does not penalise if it is a customer coordination issue (to be verified); a timeout penalises if the commitment was never met. No penalty for timeout or decline on the FPN.
  • Affirmation copy must make expectation and consequence clear, since the rules have changed.

Behaviour

How it behaves, screen by screen

The rules above decide what the journey costs. These decide how it actually runs.

Bubble

  • Hold and drag it to the bottom of the screen to dismiss the booking.
  • An FPN sound plays the moment the bubble appears. Pressing any volume button silences it — the sound stops, the bubble stays.
  • P41 starts the moment the bubble appears, not when he opens it.

FPN · address screen

  • The map at the top is a real map — it can be zoomed and panned.
  • The labels for the nearby connection and the new booking are placed dynamically and must never overlap, at any zoom level or pan position.
  • The address card carries the customer’s audio address — a play control with a waveform, and a पता नहीं समझे? link beneath it for when the recording still doesn’t help.
  • The screen has two versions — with the audio address, and one without. When the customer has not recorded one, that row is absent entirely, not shown empty or disabled.
  • Rejecting from here uses the pre-acceptance reason set.

Date screen

  • He can call the customer from this screen.
  • He can change the day, up to twice.

Rejection reasons — two sets

  • There are two groups: one shown before accepting a booking, one after. Both are drawn in Figma.
  • Collapsed by default — the sheet shows two reasons plus और वजहें. Tapping that reveals the rest.
  • After acceptance, कस्टमर से बात नहीं हो पाई and कस्टमर ने मना कर दिया appear only once he has tapped the call button at least once. Until then they are not in the list at all — he has no way of knowing either to be true, so offering them would only manufacture a reason.

Day change

  • दिन बदलें is always visible — never hidden, never greyed out.
  • Two changes are allowed. The picker states how many are left.
  • Once both are used, tapping it opens a sheet saying the day can no longer be changed — the blocked sheet. There is no third change.
  • When the day is changed from the drilldown, the day the customer originally gave always carries a tag beneath it, so he can tell it apart from the other options.

What changed in the design

  1. P41 timerStarts as soon as the CSP receives a booking, stops once he accepts.
  2. FPN screen"पता समझ नहीं आया" now sits below the address, and has been removed from the rejection reasons.
  3. Bottom sheet headlinesAll headlines follow a question format.
  4. Rejection reasonsThe two primary reasons show upfront. The rest, and the custom reason, sit under "और वजहें". Choosing the custom reason opens a text box in place, and what he writes is what gets submitted and shown back to him.
  5. Installation date screen"इस दिन ही लगाना है" sits below the date, to reinforce the commitment.
  6. Day change transitionAfter he changes the date, the screen reinforces that the install now has to happen on the new date.
  7. "Customer denied installation" copyReframed positively — we check the reason and his quality stays intact — instead of warning him about what happens if the reason is false.
