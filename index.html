Wiom Partner App · Product brief

New Connection Journey

How a partner receives a booking, accepts it, and — when he can't do it — tells us why in a way we can act on.

Figma · design final Figma · prototype start at Index HTML · low-fi prototype

Objective

Tell the partner what to do, before telling him what we know

The partner app is an execution surface. Its job is to produce predictable outcomes for Wiom, and every decision the partner makes should reach us as a signal we can verify or learn from.

The goal is more installs completed — and fewer lost to unclear information or missed coordination. Two moments decide that:

Before he accepts

He should have everything he needs to decide. A partner who declines because he wasn't told enough is a booking we lost for no reason.

After he accepts

He should finish. When he genuinely can't, we want the reason — not silence, and not a timer running out.

The problem · 10–25 August

Install tasks fail before commitment, and they fail quietly

64%of tasks die before a technician is assigned. Only ~36% ever reach assignment.
59%of all failures are a no-action timeout — the slot lapsed because the CSP never acted. Entirely pre-assignment.
~4×Pre-assignment, CSPs go silent about four times more often than they decline.
47%of post-assignment failures are a lapsed grace period, not a decline.
51%of reported post-assignment failures are customer-related — address, scheduling, cancellation, price. The largest single cause is customer-cancelled, at 38%.

What this tells us

The leverage is before commitment. Two thirds of tasks never reach a technician. Whatever we fix after assignment is working on the smaller third.

The failure mode is silence, not refusal. A no-action timeout is the single biggest way an install dies, and pre-assignment a partner is roughly four times likelier to say nothing than to say no. A decline is a usable signal; a timeout tells us nothing about why. So the design's first job is to make an honest no easy to find and free to give.

After commitment, silence is a strategy. Partners let the grace period lapse rather than decline, because declining is visibly penalised and lapsing feels like it isn't. It is the same outcome for Wiom and — under the rules we have set — the same cost to him. The app has to say that out loud, and offer a cheaper honest route: change the day, or let Wiom take the customer.

Half of late failures were knowable early. When a partner does report a post-assignment failure, about half are customer problems — the address, the schedule, a cancellation. Those are pre-commitment questions surfacing after commitment, which is exactly what the booking screen and the Wiom outreach path are built to catch.

Customer-cancelled being the single largest reported cause (38%) matters twice: it is the most common failure a partner reports, and under our rules it is not his fault and costs him nothing. If he does not know that, he has every reason to keep hiding it in a timeout.

The principle

The ✓ splits the journey in two

Everything rests on one line: before the partner accepts, every exit is free. After he accepts, leaving costs him.

So the address screen carries everything he needs to decide, including the money — it is the last moment saying no is free. Everything after it is built to help him finish, and every way out offers a way to save the job first.

Free Before acceptance

Declining costs nothing. The reason is collected purely so we learn where he can and can't work.

Costs After acceptance

Abandoning affects his install SLA — the metric tied to his earnings. So every reason opens a recovery before the decline completes.

The partner is also protected where the failure isn't his: if the customer cancels, nothing is deducted. Changing the install day is free, up to twice.

The flows

Eight paths, one hub

Every flow starts and ends at the same index screen, so any one of them can be reviewed on its own.

FLOW 0

The ask arrives

free

A pink bubble floats over whatever the partner is doing, showing only the distance to his nearest existing connection. Long-pressing offers हटाएँ or रिमाइंडर. If he dismisses the booking rather than declining it, it appears in his home feed with a 4-hour countdown — and tapping that card reopens the booking screen, not a job detail, because he hasn't accepted anything yet.

FLOW 1

Accept and install

  1. Booking screen — map with straight-line distance, the payout, landmark, address, a voice note of the directions, and his existing connections nearby. Two circular buttons: ✕ and ✓.
  2. Day — the customer's chosen date, the customer's name and a call button, and the option to change the day.
  3. Team — himself or an installer.
  4. Confirmation — (नाम) को यह कनेक्शन भेज दिया, then the job detail and back to the feed.
FLOW 2

Declining before acceptance

free

The ✕ opens a short reason list — cable can't reach, address unclear, no NetBox, on leave today. Each reason opens its own next step rather than ending the booking immediately, and the sheet says plainly that nothing is deducted.

FLOW 3

Changing the install day

free ×2

The partner picks a different day and the job continues on it. This is deliberately the cheapest tool in the journey: we would rather have an honest date early than a missed one later, because that is what lets us set the customer's expectation in time.

FLOW 4

When the customer can't be reached

Wiom acts

The app can only report that he pressed call — the IVR never tells us whether anyone answered — so the screen says how many times he called and nothing more. An escalation is always available: Wiom reaches the customer by SMS, WhatsApp and call, and connects them when they pick up. The booking stays with him while that happens.

FLOW 5

Leaving after acceptance

affects SLA

This is where installs are actually lost, so it carries the journey's only interrupt: before the reason list, he is offered the free day change. Only if he still wants out does he pick a reason — and each reason opens its own recovery first.

FLOW 6

Cancelling once a team is assigned

affects SLA

The exit moves into the overflow menu and names its consequence. It never disappears — a partner who can't find the way out goes silent instead, and silence costs him the same while telling us nothing.

FLOW 7

When the customer declines the day

A push tells him the customer didn't accept, and the job detail explains what happened.

Reasons

Every reason opens a door

A reason list is friction on a screen the partner wants to leave, so each option earns its place by triggering something real. Reasons only appear where he could actually know the answer — a partner on the booking screen has no customer contact, so he is never asked about the customer there.

Still open

Decisions this design needs

  1. How long does Wiom try before releasing the job? An open-ended wait relocates the uncertainty instead of removing it. A defined window — 48 hours is the working proposal — after which the booking either revives or becomes free to release without penalty.
  2. Is a "customer refused" claim verified? Verification protects the signal but tells the partner he isn't trusted. The proposal is to verify quietly and never surface it as suspicion — Wiom simply says it will speak to the customer.
  3. What happens when the 4-hour hold expires? The dismiss sheet promises the connection goes to another partner. There is no screen for that moment yet. Recommended: the card dims in place with the reason, consistent with never deleting a connection.
  4. Does a Wiom-verified unreachable customer count as no-fault? Proposed as a third no-fault case alongside "declined before accepting" and "customer cancelled". It cannot be gamed because the partner can never claim it — only Wiom can grant it.
  5. What happens after the two free day changes? Whether a third change is blocked, or allowed with an SLA consequence.
  6. Is the day the partner picks final, or does the customer confirm it? The design currently shows a confirmation step and a "customer declined your day" path. Removing that step would simplify the journey considerably, but it changes what we promise the customer. This needs an explicit answer before build.
